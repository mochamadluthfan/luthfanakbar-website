Paper

What Does a Green Hydrogen Mandate Cost?

Evidence from the Intermountain Power Project

Submitted for peer review · International Journal of Hydrogen Energy (Elsevier) · 2026

Overview

This paper studies the cost of producing hydrogen at the Intermountain Power Project under a renewable-only requirement. Using roughly 17,500 hours of wholesale electricity price, weather, and grid-carbon data, I compare a grid-connected pathway with a renewable-only pathway supported by hydrogen storage.

Key Findings

  • The renewable-only pathway carries a $4.37/kg (113%) cost premium over the grid-connected pathway.
  • The premium is driven mainly by lower electrolyzer utilization, not by storage cost.
  • The analysis implies a carbon abatement cost of roughly $510 per tonne of CO2.

Method & Analysis

The model compares hydrogen production configurations at a fixed hydrogen output level: a grid-connected pathway and a renewable-only ("island") pathway required to meet the project's green hydrogen mandate, with hydrogen storage used to bridge periods of low renewable output. Electricity costs, weather, and grid carbon intensity are drawn from roughly 17,500 hours of historical data.

Data & Sources

Hourly wholesale electricity price and grid-carbon-intensity data, and renewable technology cost assumptions from the U.S. National Renewable Energy Laboratory's Annual Technology Baseline.

Limitations

Results depend on the specific project configuration, technology-cost assumptions, and historical price data used. They should be read as evidence from this project, not as a general finding for all green hydrogen mandates.
